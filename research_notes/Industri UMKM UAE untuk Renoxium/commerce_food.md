# Commerce and food SME industries in the UAE (2025-2026) — profiles for Renoxium ranking

Research date: 2026-11-18. Method: ~28 web searches/fetches. Reliability legend used below:
- **[P]** primary/official or press release of the data owner (WAM, Dubai Media Office, Dubai Chamber, company results, vendor's own page).
- **[N]** established news outlet.
- **[B]** vendor/consultancy/agency blog or paywalled market-research teaser — treat as indicative only.
- **[S]** figure came from a search-result summary; the page itself was not opened, so the exact URL attribution may be imperfect.

Pages I actually opened and read: Zawya/EZDubai release, Khaleej Times 2020 commission article, Gulf News July 2026 food-establishment article, Rasmal/24SEVEN baqala article. Everything else is [S] unless stated.

## 0. Cross-cutting: market size, tools and prices, payments, regulation (applies to all five industries)

### Takeaway
UAE e-commerce is a verified AED 42.2bn market (2025) growing ~10%/yr, and roughly a third of UAE small businesses run mostly on social media rather than a site. Incumbent tools are either percentage-based (Zbooni 3.5%, marketplaces 5-27%+, aggregators ~15-35%) or USD-priced SaaS that needs a separate UAE gateway (Shopify), so Renoxium's AED 99/299 flat tiers sit at or below the low end of what these owners already pay.

### Cited Findings
**Market size**
- UAE e-commerce reached AED 42.2bn (USD 11.5bn) in 2025, up from AED 17.6bn in 2020 (19% CAGR); forecast 9.9% CAGR 2026-2030 to ~AED 67.2bn by 2030; ~15.7% of total retail sales in 2025, forecast >20% by 2030. Apparel and footwear was the largest vertical at AED 11.4bn, followed by consumer electronics and home care. EZDubai 6th annual e-commerce report (data credited to Euromonitor in earlier editions) [P, opened] — [Zawya/EZDubai](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/research-studies/ezdubai-logistics-hub-uae-e-commerce-market-size-reaches-aed-422bln-in-2025-1286875); also [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c2h35yn-uae-e-commerce-market-size-reaches-aed422-billion)
- UAE social-commerce market size estimates conflict by an order of magnitude: Ken Research USD 3.2bn (base year 2024) [B,S] — [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/united-arab-emirates-social-commerce-market); Mordor Intelligence USD 32.7bn in 2025 [B,S] — [Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/uae-social-commerce-market). The Mordor figure exceeds EZDubai's total e-commerce figure by ~3x, so it uses a different (much wider) definition and should not be quoted next to the AED 42.2bn number.
- The UAE added ~250,000 new companies in 2025 (Ministry of Economy, all sectors) [N,S] — [The National, 6 Jan 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2026/01/06/uae-adds-250000-more-companies-in-2025-as-llc-rules-simplified)
- Dubai issued ~19,000 of ~40,000 new UAE business licences in Q1 2025 (Ministry of Economy data via media) [N,S] — [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Buzz/Dubai-Accounts-for-59-of-UAE-Business-Licences-in-Q1-2025)

**Digitalisation / channel mix**
- GoDaddy 2025 Global Entrepreneurship Survey (UAE small businesses): 25% run their business primarily through their own website, 32% operate mostly on social media; on sales channels 38% sell directly through social media, 23% via online stores or marketplaces, 32% in person [S; outlet attribution uncertain] — [TahawulTech](https://www.tahawultech.com/?p=108859)
- Zbooni UAE SME survey (2021, >200 SMEs — dated, small sample, vendor-run): Instagram ranked #1 channel for growing online sales, WhatsApp #2 (44.2% called it essential for online sales), own branded website #3 (38.2%) [P-vendor,S] — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/uae/en/story/ZAWYA20210824070203/); [mid-east.info](https://mid-east.info/zbooni-reveals-results-of-nationwide-uae-sme-survey-report)

**Payments / cash on delivery**
- Payment-method mix of UAE e-commerce 2025 attributed to Worldpay: digital wallets 35%, credit card 27%, debit card 15%, bank transfer (Aani) 10%, cash 7%, other 6% [B,S] — [Antom](https://www.antom.com/markets/uae)
- Other sources put COD at ~25-30% of orders by count (2026 logistics blog; market-research teaser) [B,S] — [SamVertex](https://samvertex-web.pages.dev/blog/cod-logistics-uae/); [Scotts International](https://www.scotts-international.com/united-arab-emirates-payment-market-share-analysis-industry-trends-statistics-growth-forecasts-2026-2031,t2167595/52). Conflict: 7% (value share, Worldpay) vs 25-30% (order share, blogs) — different measures, neither verified at source.
- Checkout.com: COD use has declined ~60% since 2020; 62% of UAE consumers plan to increase online shopping next year [P-vendor,S] — [Zawya/Checkout.com](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/research-and-studies/uae-among-the-worlds-most-dynamic-and-fast-moving-digital-economies-checkoutcom-report-jg7wkf2v)

**Store builders and their UAE prices**
- Shopify: billed in USD; Basic USD 39/month (USD 29 on annual billing, ~AED 107-143), Shopify USD 105 (~AED 385), Advanced USD 399 (~AED 1,465); 2.0% extra transaction fee on Basic when using a third-party gateway; Shopify Payments historically not available in the UAE so merchants add Telr/PayTabs/Checkout.com [B,S] — [Global Media Insight](https://www.globalmediainsight.com/blog/shopify-uae/); [Shopify UAE pricing page (Arabic)](https://www.shopify.com/ae-ar/pricing)
- One vendor blog claims that since 26 Aug 2026 Shopify Payments accepts UAE cards, but only on Advanced and Plus plans — single unverified source [B,S] — [EasySell](https://easysellapp.com/blogs/wiki/uae-ecommerce-shopify-market-entry-guide-2026)
- Salla: free plan AED 0; Salla Basic ~AED 1,380/year (~AED 115/month), no platform transaction fee [B,S] — [StoreStarter](https://storestarter.pages.dev/ae-en/best-ecommerce-platform/best-ecommerce-platform-uae)
- Zbooni (UAE WhatsApp-commerce/payment links): no sign-up or monthly fee on basic package, 3.5% + VAT per order (AED 3.68 on a AED 100 order) [B,S] — [Meydan FZ guide](https://www.meydanfz.ae/blog/how-to-sell-on-zbooni); 3.5% per transaction also in [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/business/money/zbooni-the-uae-payment-service-helping-micro-businesses-process-transactions-1.852272)
- Noon marketplace: third-party guides give commission of ~4-27% by category (some say 5-20%); Noon's own help centre lists apparel 27% and footwear 27% (FBN, from 1 Dec 2024), bags 25%, luggage 20%; refurbished mobiles 5-10%; minimum referral fee AED 1/item; storage AED 0.2-5; FBP shipping AED 10 on orders under AED 100; fees exclude VAT; FBP referral fees were revised effective 1 Sep 2025 [P for Noon pages, S] — [Noon Help Center FBN fees](https://helpcenter.noon.partners/en/category/fulfilled-by-noon-fbn/fulfilled-by-noon-fbn-fees-in-uae); [Noon FBP fees](https://helpcenter.noon.partners/en/category/fulfilled-by-partner-fbp/fulfilled-by-partner-fbp-fees-in-uae); [Meydan FZ guide](https://www.meydanfz.ae/blog/ecommerce-start-selling-on-noon)

**Regulation (general)**
- Licensing: some emirates issue a standalone "individual eCommerce licence", others a normal commercial licence with an e-commerce activity [P,S] — [u.ae e-commerce page](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/business/managing-and-growing-your-business/ecommerce)
- Dubai DED/DET "e-Trader" licence (launched 2017) was created to regulate home-based sellers on social media; single owner, no physical shop, no visas; applicants register their social media accounts. Eligibility is reported inconsistently (one source: originally Emiratis/GCC nationals only; another: Dubai residents) [B,S] — [Virtuzone](https://virtuzone.com/blog/e-trader-license-dubai/); [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/e-trader-licence-dubai)
- Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2023 on trading through modern technology means is the current federal e-commerce law (secondary sources only; decree text not retrieved) [B,S] — [Middle East Briefing](https://middleeastbriefing.com/news/?p=2973)
- Dubai Municipality publishes "Technical Guidelines for Consumer Products E-Commerce" (V2, 2024) covering online sale of regulated consumer products [P; only table of contents seen] — [Dubai Municipality PDF](https://www.dm.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/DM-HSD-GU18-ECOM2_Technical-Guidelines-for-Consumer-Products-E-Commerce_V2.pdf)

### Inferences
- Price positioning: Renoxium AED 99/month is below Shopify Basic (~AED 143 + gateway + 2% fee) and comparable to Salla Basic (~AED 115/month); the Free tier with 2% card fee undercuts Zbooni's 3.5%. AED 299 is still below Foodics Basic (AED 375) and well below ChatFood (~USD 80 = ~AED 294 starting). So price is unlikely to be the objection; trust, discovery and "who sets it up" are.
- COD support is a real requirement (order-share estimates of 25-30%), but it is declining; card + COD together, as Renoxium has, matches the market.
- Stripe operates in the UAE, so Stripe Connect is usable — this is from general knowledge, not verified in this research; the writer should confirm Stripe Connect availability for UAE sole establishments/e-Trader licence holders, because many micro-sellers lack a corporate bank account.

### Gaps
- No official count of e-commerce licences in the UAE for 2025 (only the 2017-2022 e-Trader total, see industry A).
- No reliable figure for churn among small Shopify-type stores in the UAE, nor for typical monthly software spend of UAE micro-businesses. Not found.
- Zid UAE pricing, Wix and WooCommerce UAE costs, Amazon.ae referral fee table: not retrieved. Zbooni's current tiered plans (beyond the 3.5% basic) not verified on its own pricing page.
- Consumer-protection return rules (Federal Law 15/2020 and executive regulations) were not researched; no sourced statement available.
- EZDubai's full report may contain social-commerce and COD shares; only the press release was read.

## A. Small online / social-commerce retailers (fashion, abayas, perfume/oud, cosmetics, gifts, flowers, accessories) on Instagram / WhatsApp / TikTok

### Takeaway
Large and verifiably social-first population (18,013 Dubai e-Trader licences by March 2022 alone; about a third of UAE small businesses operate mostly on social media), in the biggest e-commerce vertical (apparel AED 11.4bn). Product fit is the strongest of the five because the finished UAE store template already covers it; the weak points are that alternatives are numerous and cheap, and that cosmetics/perfume sellers face Montaji product registration that software does not solve.

### Cited Findings
- Dubai issued 18,013 e-Trader licences between March 2017 and March 2022; the licence lets startups and freelancers trade online and across social media accounts [N,S] — [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/politics-economics/e-trader-licences-in-dubai-climb-to-18013-marking-the-emergence-e-commerce-as-a-key-economic-activity)
- Dubai issued 361 e-Trader licences in a single month (July; year not shown in the result, older article) [N,S] — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-issues-361-e-trader-licences-in-july?amp=1)
- Abu Dhabi: "Tajer Abu Dhabi" licences rose 17% and "Mobdea" (home-based business) licences rose 15% in Q1 2026 (growth rates only, no counts) [N,S] — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/economy/abu-dhabi-new-business-licences-jump-21-in-first-quarter-of-2026-1.500582677)
- Apparel and footwear is the largest UAE e-commerce vertical, AED 11.4bn in 2025 [P] — [Zawya/EZDubai](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/research-studies/ezdubai-logistics-hub-uae-e-commerce-market-size-reaches-aed-422bln-in-2025-1286875)
- 38% of UAE small businesses sell directly through social media vs 23% through online stores/marketplaces; 32% operate mostly on social media vs 25% primarily on their own website (GoDaddy 2025) [S] — [TahawulTech](https://www.tahawultech.com/?p=108859)
- Instagram, then WhatsApp, then own website was the ranking of channels for online sales among UAE SMEs (Zbooni, 2021) [S] — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/uae/en/story/ZAWYA20210824070203/)
- Marketplace alternative is expensive for fashion: Noon charges 27% referral on apparel and footwear (FBN) [P,S] — [Noon Help Center](https://helpcenter.noon.partners/en/category/fulfilled-by-noon-fbn/fulfilled-by-noon-fbn-fees-in-uae)
- Cosmetics, perfumes, personal care and health supplements must be registered per product (per formulation) on Dubai Municipality's Montaji system before sale in stores or online; a trade licence alone is not enough [B,S] — [BusinessDubai](https://www.businessdubai.ae/blogs/cosmetics-trading-company-setup-dubai); [Rizmona](https://www.rizmona.com/blog/cosmetics-business-dubai-complete-guide/)
- Montaji fee per product is reported inconsistently: AED 10 per product/variant (Artixio), AED 110-240 (BusinessDubai), AED 230-300 (Takween), AED 300-550 or AED 1,500-3,000 per SKU including service fees (Dubai Business Services); validity reported as 5 years by one source and annual by another [B,S] — [Artixio](https://www.artixio.com/cosmetic-product-regulation-and-registration-in-uae/); [Takween](https://takweenadvisory.ae/en/cosmetics-product-registration-dubai); [Meydan FZ](https://www.meydanfz.ae/how-to-get-your-products-registered-with-dubai-municipality/)
- The e-Trader licence was introduced partly to curb counterfeit goods sold on Instagram [B,S] — [Virtuzone](https://virtuzone.com/blog/e-trader-license-dubai/)

### Inferences
- Market size: tens of thousands of licensed social sellers nationally is a defensible order of magnitude (18k Dubai e-Trader licences by 2022, growing by a few hundred per month, plus Abu Dhabi Tajer/Mobdea, Sharjah Eitimad etc., plus unlicensed sellers). This is an estimate, not a sourced count.
- Pain: order taking in DMs, manual payment links, no stock/variant tracking, no tax invoice. These are standard for DM-based selling but I found no UAE survey quantifying them — treat as reasoned, not evidenced.
- Alternatives are not weak: Zbooni (3.5%, WhatsApp-native, UAE-based), Salla (free/AED 115), Shopify, Instagram/TikTok native checkout and Noon/Amazon all target this segment. Renoxium's differentiators would be flat fee, Arabic RTL out of the box, COD, WhatsApp sign-in, and the AI-chat build (no theme/app configuration). Shopify's lack of a native UAE gateway on low plans is a concrete weakness to cite.
- Reach: owners are literally reachable on Instagram/WhatsApp/TikTok and through e-Trader/Tajer licensing channels and business-setup consultancies — easiest of the five to reach.
- Fit: existing "store-uae" template (catalog, variants, cart, discount codes, tax invoices, COD) is a direct fit. Useful variations: abaya/tailoring made-to-measure form (measurements + deposit), perfume/oud sample sizes and gift sets, flower/gift delivery-date and time-slot picker with card message, pre-order/drop model with stock countdown. Public MCP product search is a novel plus for gift and perfume discovery.
- Gap for Renoxium: no Instagram/TikTok shop catalog sync and no courier integration were mentioned in the feature list; Tabby/Tamara BNPL is common in UAE fashion checkout (general knowledge, not sourced here).

### Gaps
- No post-2022 total for e-Trader licences; no UAE-wide count of social sellers.
- No data on share of these sellers already on Shopify/Salla/Zid, or on store churn.
- TikTok Shop UAE status and fees not researched.
- Whether e-Trader licence holders can open Stripe accounts was not checked.

## B. Restaurants, cafes, cloud kitchens and home-based food businesses

### Takeaway
Very large and well-counted base (29,303 food establishments in Dubai in H1 2026; ~24,600-26,400 in Abu Dhabi, 98% SMEs) with a loud, long-standing pain: aggregator commissions commonly quoted at 25-35% of order value. But direct-ordering alternatives are mature (ChatFood, Zyda, Foodics Online, Blink, GrubTech) and aggregators own the demand, so Renoxium's fit is real but competition and "who brings the orders" limit it.

### Cited Findings
**Counts**
- Dubai had 29,303 food establishments, with 1,898 new ones opened in H1 2026 (~10.5 per day), per Dubai Municipality via Al Bayan [N, opened; published 17 Jul 2026] — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/retail/more-than-10-new-food-businesses-are-launching-in-dubai-every-day-1.500611405). No breakdown by type (restaurant/cafe/cloud kitchen/grocery/bakery) is given; "food establishments" includes all food premises, not only restaurants.
- H1 2025: ~2,336 new food establishments opened in Dubai and 34,700 inspection visits [P,S] — [Dubai Media Office, 30 Jul 2025](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/july/30-07/dubai-municipality-conducts-34700-food-inspections-in-the-first-half-of-2025)
- Early 2024: "over 26,000" food establishments in Dubai [P,S] — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/b1qrfvc-over-26000-food-establishments-operate-dubai)
- DET issued 1,200 new restaurant licences in 2024 (Dubai Gastronomy Industry Report) [P,S] — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bj3b5m5-dubai-ranks-2nd-globally-gastronomy-capital-with)
- Abu Dhabi: 24,594 active F&B licences as of Sept 2025, a 42% surge in registrations (ADCCI report) [P,S] — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bykkvfv-abu-dhabi-sees-42-surge-fb-business-registrations). A separate ADAFSA-sourced report gives 26,360 active food establishments with SMEs at 98% of the sector [S; date and exact URL uncertain] — [WAM/ADAFSA Golden List](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bmcljmn-adafsa-launches-golden-list-incentivise-excellence)
- ADAFSA closed 55 food outlets in 2025 and 14 more in early 2026 for safety violations [N,S] — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/health/abu-dhabi-shuts-69-food-outlets-in-18-months-over-serious-safety-violations-1.500568093)

**Delivery market and aggregators**
- UAE online food delivery market size estimates conflict: IMARC USD 793.9m in 2025 (USD 1.82bn by 2034); Arabian Business headline cites a USD 1.36bn market; The Report Cubes USD 10.86bn in 2026 [B,S] — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/uae-online-food-delivery-market-statistics); [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/industries/transport/dubai-rules-36bn-online-food-delivery); [The Report Cubes](https://www.thereportcubes.com/report-store/online-food-delivery-market-uae). The low figures are clearly platform revenue or a narrow definition; see next bullet for scale.
- talabat (group, 8 MENA markets): FY2025 GMV ~USD 9.4-9.5bn (+27-28%), GCC GMV USD 7.7bn, revenue ~USD 3.9bn, ~84,000 active partners (+22% YoY) — group-wide, not UAE-only [P,S] — [Zawya/talabat Q4 2025 results](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/talabat-reports-resilient-results-for-q4-2025-announces-disciplined-investment-cycle-to-drive-long-term-growth-mue1ykjm)
- Keeta (Meituan) launched in Dubai in Sept 2025, reached all seven emirates within 90 days, and claims >10,000 restaurant/merchant brands and >40,000 locations after one year (partner content, company claim) [N-advertorial,S] — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/keeta-marks-first-anniversary-in-the-uae-celebrating-nationwide-growth-and-local-impact?amp=1)
- Mobile food-delivery orders in the UAE and Saudi Arabia rose 30% in H1 2025; market expected to grow 10.2% a year [N,S] — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-online-food-delivery-market-likely-to-expand-at-102-a-year)

**Commission pain (named sources)**
- "Aggregators charge between 25 to 30 per cent as a commission on orders" — Gaurav Varma, CEO Royal Orchid Group, Abu Dhabi; "up to 35 per cent on an order" — Bhanu Pratap Rathore, GrowthX Advisors; additional costs: card commission, restaurant-funded discounts, paid visibility/advertising, delivery charges; some restaurants offer discounts for ordering direct; calls for a commission cap, none enacted [N, opened; article dated 26 Apr 2020 — old, Covid-era] — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/local/commission-rates-by-delivery-apps-proving-costly-to-uae-restaurants)
- More recent but lower-quality sources: talabat commission 15-35% per order [B,S] — [Menuviel](https://blog.menuviel.com/?p=9319); 30% "average" with effective cost 32-35% of gross order value after extra fees, based on operator interviews [B,S] — [Percee Digital](https://percee-digital.pages.dev/insights/restaurant-delivery-margins); 15-25% per a free-zone guide [B,S] — [UAE Freezone Finder](https://uaefreezonefinder.com/uae-food-delivery-app-aggregator-guide-2026/). Platform rate cards are not public and vary by contract.
- One guide claims Deliveroo exited the UAE in 2024; other 2025-26 sources still list Deliveroo UAE commissions (22-28%). Unverified and likely wrong — do not repeat without checking [B,S] — [UAE Freezone Finder](https://uaefreezonefinder.com/uae-food-delivery-app-aggregator-guide-2026/)

**Tools and prices**
- Foodics (POS/restaurant OS) UAE: Starter AED 199, Basic AED 375, Advanced AED 556 per month billed annually; hardware quoted separately [P for Advanced, S for others] — [Foodics UAE pricing](https://www.foodics.com/foodics-pricing-uae/). A competitor claims Foodics takes 1-2% per online order (unverified) [B,S] — [DineOpen](https://www.dineopen.com/alternatives/foodics)
- ChatFood (UAE-born direct ordering, now a Deliverect company): no commission on orders, flat monthly/annual fee, only card-processing fee on top; orders via own site, WhatsApp, Instagram; contracts auto-renew [P,S; pricing page returned 503 when opened] — [ChatFood pricing](https://chatfood.io/pricing). Starting price ~USD 80/month with 30-day trial per an older partner page [B,S] — [Unilever Food Solutions Arabia](https://unileverfoodsolutionsarabia.com/en/chef-inspiration/business-support/food-delivery/food-delivery-should-go-direct.html)

**Regulation**
- Selling home-cooked food in Dubai requires a trade licence (e.g. e-Trader/DET) plus Dubai Municipality food permit; unlicensed home food selling is not permitted and Dubai Municipality has publicly reminded residents of this [N,S] — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-municipality-kids-selling-homemade-food-licence-required); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/uae-can-i-sell-food-that-i-have-cooked-at-home-how-you-can-become-a-food-entrepreneur-1.1617031130656)
- Home food permit process as described by a Dubai culinary school: register on Dubai Municipality FoodWatch, submit Emirates ID, tenancy/Ejari, kitchen layout, food-safety training certificate; home inspection; home kitchens limited to light/traditional/sweet items; fees ~AED 1,000-3,000 [B,S] — [ICCA Dubai](https://www.iccadubai.ae/stockpot/home-food-license-dubai-how-to-legally-start-your-home-based-food-business)

### Inferences
- Market size is the largest countable base here: ~55,000 licensed food establishments in Dubai + Abu Dhabi alone (29,303 + ~24,600-26,400), though this includes groceries, bakeries, caterers and factories; restaurants/cafes are a subset of unknown size.
- Pain severity is high and widely voiced (30% of revenue vs a typical restaurant margin), and Keeta's 2025 entry intensifies platform competition (more discounting funded by restaurants) — but it also means aggregators are subsidising demand, making it harder to move customers to direct ordering.
- Alternatives are strong, not weak: ChatFood, Zyda, Blink, GrubTech, Foodics Online, plus POS-integrated ordering. Renoxium lacks POS/kitchen-display integration, aggregator menu sync and a rider fleet. Its price (AED 99-299 flat vs ChatFood ~AED 294+ and Foodics AED 199-556) and the 2%-or-0% fee are advantages for the smallest operators (cafeterias, home kitchens, single-outlet cafes) who cannot justify a POS suite.
- Best-fit sub-segment: home-based food businesses, tiffin/meal-plan services and small cafes that currently take orders on WhatsApp — pre-order menus, weekly meal subscriptions (scheduled jobs), delivery slots, COD, realtime order status. Template needed: "restaurant direct-order menu" (categories, modifiers/add-ons, pickup vs delivery zones and fees, opening hours, order status board with realtime updates, WhatsApp order notification) and a "meal plan / tiffin subscription" variant.
- Reach: restaurant owners are harder to reach than Instagram sellers (busy, many managed by groups), but are concentrated and listed (aggregator listings, Google Maps); home food sellers are reachable on Instagram/WhatsApp.

### Gaps
- No UAE-only talabat partner count or GMV; no count of cloud kitchens or licensed home food businesses.
- No verified 2025-2026 commission rate from a named UAE restaurant owner or from platform terms; the best named quotes are from 2020.
- Zyda, Blink, GrubTech pricing not found. ChatFood's current plan prices not confirmed (page unavailable).
- No evidence found on payout-delay complaints.
- ADAFSA rules for home food businesses in Abu Dhabi not retrieved.
- Reddit owner statements were not retrieved.

## C. Catering and bakery / sweets / custom-cake businesses

### Takeaway
Sizeable market by value (UAE catering ~USD 4.1bn in 2025, dominated by large contract caterers) but the SME custom-order segment (home bakers, cake studios, sweets, small event caterers) is not counted anywhere. The pain is specific and poorly served by generic stores and food aggregators — quote/custom-order forms, deposits, lead times and delivery date slots — which makes product fit good if Renoxium ships a pre-order template; evidence for this segment is mostly inferential.

### Cited Findings
- UAE catering services market valued at ~USD 4.1bn in 2025, projected USD 6.25bn by 2032; another outlook gives 6% CAGR 2025-30, driven partly by air passenger traffic (i.e. includes airline/industrial catering) [B,S] — [MarkNtel Advisors](https://www.marknteladvisors.com/press-release/uae-catering-services-market-growth); [finanzen.net release](https://finanzen.net/nachricht/aktien/uae-catering-services-market-set-to-grow-6-cagr-through-2030-key-trends-growth-drivers-competitive-shift-and-leading-companies-royal-catering-services-emirates-catering-services-adnh-cate-14769446)
- The catering industry is fragmented; top five players hold ~45% share (2026) [B,S] — [The Report Cubes](https://www.thereportcubes.com/public/report-store/uae-catering-services-market)
- UAE bakery: customised cakes, seasonal launches and premium celebration cakes are cited as growth drivers; "domestic and home baking" is described as the smallest segment [B,S] — [Nexdigm](https://www.nexdigm.com/market-research/insights/blog/uae-bakery-products-industry/)
- Home bakers need the same two tracks as other home food sellers in Dubai: trade licence plus Dubai Municipality home food permit; home kitchens are limited to light/traditional/sweet items (which covers cakes and sweets) [B,S] — [ICCA Dubai, home baking licence](https://www.iccadubai.ae/stockpot/home-baking-license-dubai-how-to-legally-start-and-grow-your-home-bakery)
- Bakeries, caterers and sweet shops are included within the 29,303 Dubai food establishments and the ~24,600 Abu Dhabi F&B licences; no sub-count is published in the sources found [N] — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/retail/more-than-10-new-food-businesses-are-launching-in-dubai-every-day-1.500611405); [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bykkvfv-abu-dhabi-sees-42-surge-fb-business-registrations)
- Aggregator commissions (same 15-35% range) apply to bakeries listed on talabat/Keeta/Careem [B,S] — [Menuviel](https://blog.menuviel.com/?p=9319)

### Inferences
- Market size for the SME slice is unknown; plausibly low thousands of businesses UAE-wide (bakeries, sweet shops, cake studios, small caterers, licensed home bakers) — an estimate without a source.
- Pain (reasoned, not sourced): custom cakes and catering are quote-based and date-bound, so aggregators' instant-menu model fits badly; orders are negotiated in WhatsApp/Instagram DMs with photos, flavours, sizes, inscription, date, and a deposit by bank transfer or payment link. Errors, no-shows and capacity overbooking are the typical failure modes. Ramadan/Eid, National Day and wedding seasons create peaks where pre-order capacity management matters.
- Weakness of alternatives: Shopify/Salla-type stores handle fixed SKUs, not "request a quote then pay deposit"; restaurant direct-ordering tools handle instant menus, not lead-time orders. Zbooni payment links are the closest current fit (3.5%).
- Fit with Renoxium: strong for a custom app — form builder via chat, deposit by card (Stripe) with balance on delivery (COD), delivery date/slot capacity, admin panel as order calendar, scheduled reminders, Arabic. Template needed: "pre-order cake form with deposit" (size/flavour/design upload/inscription, date picker with lead-time and daily capacity, deposit %, pickup/delivery) and a "catering quote request" variant (headcount, menu packages, per-head pricing, quote approval, deposit invoice with VAT).
- Reach: owners are highly active on Instagram and reachable through home-business licensing programmes (e-Trader, Mobdea, Tajer) and baking-supply shops/culinary schools.

### Gaps
- No count of bakeries, sweet shops, caterers or licensed home bakers in any emirate.
- No UAE evidence (survey, news, forum) quantifying how custom-cake/catering orders are taken or how often deposits are used; no willingness-to-pay data.
- No UAE-specific vertical tools for bakeries/caterers identified; did not check whether any exist.

## D. General trading / wholesale / B2B distributors (Dragon Mart, Deira traders, re-export)

### Takeaway
Probably the largest licence category in the UAE by count, but I could not find a current official number for general-trading or wholesale licences, and almost no evidence about their digital tools or pain. Fit with Renoxium is conceptually good (B2B portal with customer-specific prices, quotes, repeat orders, VAT invoices) but requires a new template, and owners are hard to reach online and conduct business largely by WhatsApp and relationships.

### Cited Findings
- Dragon Mart 1 and 2 (International City, Dubai — not Deira) together have more than 5,000 shops; one report says >1,700 Chinese traders operate them; Dragon Mart 2 opened with ~570 shops and 520 booths [B/N,S] — [Bayut guide](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/dragon-mart-dubai/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/watch-chinese-expats-at-dubais-dragon-mart-a-bustling-community-1.89994145)
- Dragon Mart has no central online store; some traders sell online individually (visitor-guide statement, weak source) [B,S] — [Dubai-Online](https://www.dubai-online.com/malls/dragonmart/)
- Dubai Chamber of Commerce added 53,838 new member companies in the first nine months of 2025 (+4% YoY) and 35,532 in H1 2025; Indian-owned businesses were the largest group of new non-UAE members, Pakistan second (4,281 in H1 2025). All sectors, not trading only [P,S] — [Dubai Chamber](https://www.dubaichambercommerce.com/e/dcc_news_detailed_page/29577/4064323); [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bl5qbc0-over-35000-new-companies-join-dubai-chamber)
- Historically, "general trade" led the list of top licensed activities in DED monthly reports, and Bur Dubai and Deira were the largest licensing districts (older DED data; e.g. Bur Dubai 31,604 and Deira 14,022 licences in one reporting period, undated) [N,S] — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/business%2Fcommercial-sector-tops-dubai-licences-in-august-1.131795); [Arabian Business tag page](https://arabianbusiness.com/tags/business-registration-and-licensing)
- DET's October 2025 Free Zone Mainland Operating Permit includes trading among eligible activities (signals continued growth of trading firms selling onshore) [P,S] — [Dubai Media Office](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/october/08-10/dubai-launches-free-zone-mainland-operating-permit)

### Inferences
- Market size: trading is very likely the single biggest SME category (Dubai Chamber membership is trade-heavy; UAE added ~250k companies in 2025), but a defensible number of general-trading/wholesale SMEs could not be sourced. The writer should present this as "tens of thousands, exact figure not found".
- Pain (reasoned, not sourced): price lists sent as PDF/Excel/WhatsApp images; customer-specific pricing and credit terms; quotes and proforma invoices built manually; stock availability asked by phone; repeat orders re-typed; VAT tax invoices and statements. Re-exporters also deal in USD and multiple currencies and with export documents, which Renoxium's AED-centric feature list does not cover.
- Weakness of alternatives: B2B marketplaces (e.g. Tradeling, Alibaba) take discovery but not the trader's own customer relationships; Shopify B2B features sit on the top-tier plan (general knowledge, not sourced here); ERP/accounting tools (Zoho, Tally, Odoo) are common but are not customer-facing ordering portals. So a cheap private ordering portal is plausibly under-served — but this is unverified.
- Fit with Renoxium: medium-high technically, since a custom app with login (WhatsApp sign-in), per-customer price tiers, quote requests, reorder button, tax invoices and an admin panel is exactly what chat-built custom apps are good at; card payment and COD are less relevant (bank transfer, cheques/PDCs, credit terms dominate — general knowledge). Template needed: "B2B wholesale portal" (gated catalog, customer groups with price lists, MOQ and case packs, RFQ to quote to order, credit-limit and outstanding balance view, statement/tax invoice PDFs, multi-currency display, Arabic/English, optionally Chinese for Dragon Mart).
- Reach: hard. Owners are offline-first, multilingual (Hindi/Urdu/Malayalam/Chinese/Arabic/Farsi), and sceptical of software; channels would be accountants/PRO firms, business-setup consultancies and trade associations rather than social ads.

### Gaps
- No current count of general trading or wholesale licences (DET/Dubai Economic Register activity-level data not found). No Deira-specific trader count, no re-export SME count.
- No evidence found on digital adoption, software spend or stated pain of UAE traders; no Tradeling/B2B-marketplace fee data.
- No named-owner quotes. This profile is the least evidenced of the five.

## E. Groceries, minimarts (baqala) and specialty food importers

### Takeaway
Baqalas are numerous (81% of UAE grocery retailers by count, in a USD 40.5bn grocery market) and strikingly under-digitised (70% without a connected POS), but their orders arrive by phone/WhatsApp from the surrounding buildings and their margins and tech appetite are low, while quick-commerce apps are well funded. Specialty food importers and niche grocers (organic, ethnic, gourmet, dates, coffee, honey) are a smaller but much better-fitting sub-segment for the existing store template.

### Cited Findings
- UAE grocery retail market projected to exceed USD 40.5bn in 2024; 81% of grocery retailers are baqalas (stores of 50 sqm or less); 60% of surveyed residents visit a baqala weekly (convenience of location 59%, specific products 31%, opening hours 27%); fewer than 1% of baqalas have advanced fintech and 70% lack a connected POS. Source: retail-tech firm 24SEVEN, three months of research (a vendor with an interest in the finding) [B, opened; article dated 3 May 2024] — [Rasmal](https://www.rasmal.com/inside-the-uaes-40-5-billion-diverse-grocery-retail-ecosystem); also [SME10X](https://www.sme10x.com/technology/uae-grocery-market-set-to-surpass-40-billion-with-small-stores-playing-a-key-role)
- The 24SEVEN research drew on data from over 5,000 grocery outlets across the UAE (sample, not a census) [B,S] — [SME10X](https://www.sme10x.com/technology/uae-grocery-market-set-to-surpass-40-billion-with-small-stores-playing-a-key-role)
- Al Ain alone had ~900 licensed grocery businesses registered with DED (old article, mid-2010s) [N,S] — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/uae%2Fgovernment%2Ffirst-baqala-grocery-launches-in-al-ain-1.1945147)
- Abu Dhabi regulates baqalas through a branded upgrade programme (standardised refrigeration, shelving, decor) run by the food control authority; dozens closed rather than upgrade [N,S] — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/lifestyle/after-dozens-close-how-abu-dhabi-s-quirky-corner-shops-have-taken-stock-1.293185)
- Chain competition: ENOC's ZOOM operates 240+ convenience stores [N,S] — [Images RetailME](https://www.imagesretailme.com/zoom-announces-150-growth-plan-uae-2025/)
- Quick-commerce pressure: talabat's group GMV (food + grocery/"tMart") grew 27-28% in 2025 to ~USD 9.4-9.5bn; Keeta entered in Sept 2025 [P,S] — [Zawya/talabat](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/talabat-reports-resilient-results-for-q4-2025-announces-disciplined-investment-cycle-to-drive-long-term-growth-mue1ykjm)
- Noon charges up to 40% for categories fulfilled from its grocery warehouses (home and kitchen etc.), illustrating marketplace cost for small importers [P,S] — [Noon grocery-warehouse fees](https://support.noon.partners/portal/en/kb/articles/noon-grocery-fees-in-uae)
- Food establishments (which include groceries and food trading/import businesses) total 29,303 in Dubai (H1 2026) and ~24,600 active F&B licences in Abu Dhabi (Sept 2025) [N/P] — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/retail/more-than-10-new-food-businesses-are-launching-in-dubai-every-day-1.500611405); [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bykkvfv-abu-dhabi-sees-42-surge-fb-business-registrations)
- Health supplements require Montaji registration before online sale, relevant to specialty health-food importers [B,S] — [BusinessDubai](https://www.businessdubai.ae/blogs/cosmetics-trading-company-setup-dubai)

### Inferences
- Count: no official number, but "thousands" of baqalas is safe (Al Ain alone had ~900 licensed groceries a decade ago; 24SEVEN sampled 5,000+ outlets). A rough UAE-wide estimate of 5,000-10,000 is my own extrapolation, not sourced.
- Pain exists (phone/WhatsApp orders, credit "khata" books for regular customers, no stock system) but severity as felt by owners is low: free delivery by phone already works within a few buildings, baskets are small, staff turn over, and 70% have not even adopted a connected POS — a sign of low willingness to pay rather than unmet demand.
- Alternatives: aggregators' grocery verticals and dark stores compete directly for the same convenience mission; B2B restocking apps exist for baqalas (not researched). A web shop does not obviously win a baqala more orders.
- Fit: baqala — low-medium (large SKU counts need barcode/POS sync, which Renoxium's feature list lacks). Specialty importers/niche grocers — high: limited catalog, higher basket, UAE-wide delivery, repeat/subscription orders (scheduled jobs), B2B + B2C price lists, COD, Arabic; the existing store template fits with small variations (weight-based variants, expiry/batch notes, subscription reorder, wholesale price tier for restaurants/cafes).
- Reach: baqala owners are hard to reach digitally (in-person sales needed, low English/Arabic literacy among some operators — general knowledge); specialty importers are reachable via Instagram, food trade shows (Gulfood) and free-zone/setup consultancies.

### Gaps
- No official count of baqalas/groceries by emirate; the 81% and 70% figures come from one vendor study (2024).
- No data on online grocery share for small independents, or on tools they use; no willingness-to-pay evidence.
- No count of specialty food importers; food-import registration requirements (Dubai Municipality FIRS / ZAD, ADAFSA) were not researched.
