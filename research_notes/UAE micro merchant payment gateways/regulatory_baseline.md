## 1. What CBUAE rules require payment providers to collect from merchants vs individuals; is there a recognised Emirates-ID-only "individual seller" tier?

### Takeaway
No source found shows a CBUAE-defined "individual / sole seller" merchant tier with Emirates-ID-only onboarding; the CBUAE framework is risk-based CDD (identify and verify the customer from reliable independent documents, ongoing monitoring), and the "individual account" tier seen in the market is a provider-level policy choice rather than a named regulatory category. In practice providers split: Stripe requires a UAE trade licence or freelancer permit for every business type, while Nomod onboards "anyone selling without a trade license" on Emirates ID + proof of address + bank details.

### Cited Findings
- The Retail Payment Services and Card Schemes (RPSCS) Regulation is CBUAE Circular 15/2021; it sets the licensing rules for retail payment services and card schemes (snippet) — [CBUAE rulebook](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/node/1580)
- RPSCS covers nine retail payment service categories, one of which is merchant acquiring (snippet) — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/financial-services/1747336/uae-payment-service-provider-regulation-cbuae-rpscs-licensing-capital-and-compliance)
- Providers apply for one of four licence categories; a merchant-acquiring provider can apply for Category I, II or III (snippet) — [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/ckroryt8u176h0a53deo3qbrx/licensing-scheme-for-uae-payments-providers-and-card-scheme-operators)
- RPSCS effective date is reported inconsistently: the rulebook lists 6 June 2021; Mondaq and Simmons & Simmons give 15 July 2021 (snippet) — [CBUAE rulebook](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/node/1580); [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/ckroryt8u176h0a53deo3qbrx/licensing-scheme-for-uae-payments-providers-and-card-scheme-operators)
- The Stored Value Facilities (SVF) Regulation was issued in 2020 (sources give 30 September 2020 or November 2020) and widened the range of entities that can hold an SVF licence (snippet) — [CBUAE press release](https://www.centralbank.ae/en/news-and-publications/news-and-insights/press-release/cbuae-issues-a-new-regulation-on-stored-value-facilities-to-support-the-development-of-digital-payment-services-in-the-uae/)
- SVF Regulation defines CDD as "(a) identifying the Customer and verifying that Customer's identity using reliable, independent source documents, data or information, and (b) on-going due diligence" (snippet of rulebook text) — [CBUAE rulebook, SVF Regulation](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/en/entiresection/1536)
- SVF Regulation exempts small facilities where aggregate float does not exceed AED 500,000 and aggregate customers are not more than 100; this is a scope threshold for the licensing regime, not a customer KYC tier (snippet) — [CBUAE rulebook, SVF Regulation](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/en/entiresection/1536)
- CBUAE has a "Guidance for Licensed Financial Institutions on the Risks Relating to Payments" that applies to SVFs, retail payment service providers and card schemes and has a section on CDD, enhanced due diligence and ongoing monitoring (snippet; text not opened) — [CBUAE rulebook node 4368](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/node/4368)
- CBUAE published an AML/CFT/CPF thematic review of SVF, retail payment services, card scheme and payment token providers, dated May 2026 in the document; it flags non-face-to-face onboarding channels as a risk factor (snippet; PDF returned 404 when fetched) — [CBUAE thematic review](https://centralbank.ae/media/0tkpisux/thematic-review-of-providers-of-stored-value-facilities-retail-payment-services-and-card-schemes-and-payment-token-services.pdf)
- New Central Bank law: Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of 2025 repeals the 2018 Central Bank Law and the 2023 insurance decree-law and consolidates banking, payments and insurance; sources give issue date 8 September 2025 and effective date 16 September 2025 (snippet) — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/uae-enacts-new-cbuae-law-which-repeals-and-replaces-2018-law); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/uae-central-bank-issues-new-central-bank-law-consolidating-financial-sector-regulation/)
- Under the 2025 law, maximum administrative fines for institutions rise to AED 1 billion and authorised individuals can be fined up to AED 5 million; criminal provisions address unlicensed activities; CBUAE can deduct penalties directly from violators' accounts before a final judgment (snippet) — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dh1-billion-fine-central-bank-new-financial-law); [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/uae-introduces-new-central-bank-law-2025-key-changes-for-licensed-institutions/)
- The 2025 law gave affected entities a one-year transition to 16 September 2026, extendable at CBUAE discretion; no source confirming an extension was found (snippet) — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/the-new-uae-central-bank-law-expanding-the-regulatory-perimeter-for-a-digital-era)
- Stripe UAE: sole establishments must provide a "Trade license or freelancer permit issued within the UAE"; companies add memorandum of association, proof of active bank account (statement within six months) and PoA where relevant; representatives/25%+ owners provide passport and Emirates ID (plus residence visa for non-GCC residents, optional for Emirates IDs issued on or after 11 April 2022). The page defines no unlicensed-individual business type (page undated; read November 2026) — [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/uae-account-activation-requirements)
- Nomod: "Individual accounts" cover "Freelancers with freelancer permits, consultants, or anyone selling without a trade license"; documents are Emirates ID, proof of address, bank account information; a freelance permit is requested only "if available". Business accounts require a valid trade licence, business bank account, and 25%+ shareholder verification. No volume limits are stated on the page (page undated; read November 2026) — [Nomod docs](https://nomod.com/docs/account/set-up-account)
- Shopify community threads report merchants being unable to use any UAE payment gateway without a trade licence (forum posts, anecdotal) (snippet) — [Shopify community](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-can-i-use-a-payment-gateway-in-uae-without-a-trade-license/87065)
- A 2019 legal commentator noted unlicensed sellers may be unable to obtain payment terminals and so cannot accept card payments (dated 27 June 2019) — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/up-to-dh500000-fine-for-selling-without-licence-on-social-media-in-uae)

### Inferences
- "No trade license required" in provider marketing appears to mean the provider onboards the seller as a natural person under individual CDD (Emirates ID, address, bank account); it does not mean the commercial-licensing obligation (section 2) is waived. The payments rules and the trade-licensing rules are separate regimes enforced by different authorities (CBUAE vs emirate economic departments / Ministry of Economy).
- Because CBUAE CDD is described in risk-based terms rather than a fixed document list, providers have latitude to accept natural persons, but the May 2026 thematic review's focus on non-face-to-face onboarding suggests supervisory pressure on exactly the remote, ID-only flows used for individuals.
- The contrast between Stripe (licence or freelancer permit mandatory) and Nomod (Emirates ID only) indicates risk appetite, acquirer/scheme arrangements and provider licensing status, not a single regulatory rule, drive the difference.

### Gaps
- Could not read the RPSCS Regulation, SVF Regulation, the payments-risk Guidance, or the May 2026 thematic review directly (403/404/blocked host). Unverified: exact merchant due-diligence articles for merchant acquirers/payment aggregators, whether the rules explicitly require a trade licence for a "merchant", any simplified due diligence tiers, and SVF wallet balance/transaction limits by KYC level.
- No source found for a CBUAE-defined "individual / sole seller / personal" merchant onboarding tier. Its non-existence is not confirmed either.
- No source found on the current AML/CFT statute and executive regulation applying to payment providers as of 2026 (a search for a 2025 replacement AML decree-law returned nothing usable); the applicable AML law number/date is unverified.
- Whether the 16 September 2026 transition deadline under Decree-Law 6 of 2025 was extended is unverified.
- Which CBUAE licence (or sponsor-bank/acquirer arrangement) Nomod, Ziina and similar providers operate under was not researched here.

## 2. Is it legal to sell goods/services without a trade licence? Penalties and the lightest alternatives

### Takeaway
No: every source consulted says any commercial activity in the UAE, including hobby-scale and social-media selling, needs a licence, with fines cited from about AED 5,000 up to AED 500,000. The lightest legitimate routes are the Dubai DET "e-Trader" licence (about AED 1,070 plus AED 300 chamber fee per year) and the Abu Dhabi freelancer licence (about AED 790-1,200 per year), with free-zone freelance permits costing roughly AED 5,000-11,500 per year before visa costs.

### Cited Findings
Legality and penalties
- "A trade licence is required to run any kind of business in the UAE", with no exception for size or turnover, including hobby businesses where money changes hands (published 29 December 2025, updated 11 August 2026; columnist Keren Bobker, not legal advice) — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/12/29/do-i-need-to-register-a-business-for-home-based-work)
- Fines for operating without a valid licence start at around AED 5,000; other consequences include business suspension, legal action and possible blacklisting of the individual (same article, updated 11 August 2026) — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/12/29/do-i-need-to-register-a-business-for-home-based-work)
- Up to AED 500,000 fine for failing to obtain the relevant licence, per Kayaan Unwalla (DWF); basis cited is Article 6 of Dubai Law No. 13 of 2011 requiring every trading activity to be licensed by DED; possible criminal sanctions per Rhea Bindra (Davidson & Co). Dated 27 June 2019, so the figure may not be current — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/up-to-dh500000-fine-for-selling-without-licence-on-social-media-in-uae)
- A 2025 Khaleej Times list of influencer/content-creator fines repeats that selling on social media without a trade licence may lead to fines of up to AED 500,000, confiscation of goods and imprisonment (snippet) — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/life-and-living/influencer-fines-content-creator-full-list-2025)
- Federal framework: online commerce is regulated by Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2023 on Modern Technology-Based Trade; u.ae says selling online on the mainland requires a commercial licence including e-commerce activity from the emirate's economic department, and free-zone traders need a DED licence to sell into the mainland (snippet) — [u.ae e-commerce](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/business/ecommerce)
- Cabinet Resolution No. 200 of 2025 activates a graduated penalty schedule under the digital commerce law: written warning (up to 15 days to rectify), fines up to AED 100,000, and permanent closure (snippet) — [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/digital-commerce-platform-compliance-the-new-penalty-framework-for-uae-platforms/); [uaelegislation.gov.ae](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/3989/download)
- Earlier Reed Smith analysis noted the 2023 law itself set no penalties and deferred them to a Cabinet decision (consistent with the later 2025 resolution) (snippet) — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102iqny/uae-federal-law-on-modern-trading-by-technological-means/)

Dubai e-Trader (DED/DET Trader) licence
- Annual DED fee AED 1,070 plus Dubai Chamber fee AED 300; suggested for a Dubai resident providing a service/consulting from home; apply online or at the DED cafe, Palm Strip Mall, Jumeirah (updated 11 August 2026) — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/12/29/do-i-need-to-register-a-business-for-home-based-work)
- "UAE nationals and residents can apply" via the Invest in Dubai portal, licence received by email in minutes, "from Dh1,070"; it allows selling products or services through a website or social media (published 24 September 2026; only the intro was retrievable) — [Emirates 24|7](https://www.emirates247.com/uae-guide/dubai-e-trader-licence-how-to-legally-start-a-home-based-business-in-dubai-from-dh1070/5992)
- Conflict on eligibility: the u.ae page is reported as limiting the licence to UAE and GCC nationals residing in Dubai, while other guides say expat residents can register (commonly for professional/service activities) (snippet; u.ae page returned 404 when fetched) — [u.ae DED Trader](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/business/ecommerce/ded-trader-licence-from-dubai); [Emirates 24|7](https://www.emirates247.com/uae-guide/dubai-e-trader-licence-how-to-legally-start-a-home-based-business-in-dubai-from-dh1070/5992)
- Conflict on cost: Property Finder says the licence "starts at approximately AED 1,370 per year" (matches 1,070 + 300 chamber fee) (snippet) — [Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/e-trader-licence-dubai)
- Restrictions: valid one year, renewable; holder cannot open a physical shop or issue visas (snippet) — [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/e-trader-licence-dubai)

Abu Dhabi freelancer licence
- Abu Dhabi mainland freelance licence issued by Abu Dhabi Registration and Licensing Authority: AED 790-1,200 per year; estimated year-1 total with 2-year visa, medical, Emirates ID and insurance AED 8,000-14,500 (published 29 July 2026; figures compiled from third parties) — [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-freelance-licence-visa-costs-requirements-2026/)
- 2026 fee breakdown: about AED 790 organisational-entities fee, AED 10 economic register, AED 315 establishment card, AED 300 Abu Dhabi Chamber membership; applied for through TAMM with UAE PASS (snippet) — [Emirates 24|7](https://www.emirates247.com/uae-guide/abu-dhabi-freelancer-licence-guide-2026-eligibility-documents-fees-and-step-by-step-tamm-application/7132)
- Conflicting figures: older Gulf News piece cites AED 530 and a separate AED 2,500 Ministry of Economy fee; The National (November 2024) reports AED 1,070 per year (snippet) — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/how-can-i-apply-for-a-freelancer-licence-in-abu-dhabi-1.1605532573650); [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/business/money/2024/11/20/uae-freelance-licence-work)
- No office needed (work from home); documents centre on proof of expertise such as attested qualification or experience certificates; ADDED expanded the licence by 30 activities to 100 in total (August 2024) (snippet) — [ADDED](https://www.added.gov.ae/en/news-and-highlights/media-announcement/ADDED-includes-30-new-activities-to-Freelancer-Licence); [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/freelancer-licence-abu-dhabi/)

Free-zone freelance permits and MoHRE

- "Budget free zones": licence AED 4,888-6,800 per year (zones not named) — [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-freelance-licence-visa-costs-requirements-2026/)
- Sharjah Media City individual licence from AED 5,760; Dynamic Freelancer permit AED 6,500 plus VAT for people already on a sponsored visa, or AED 14,888 complete package, with a 2.49% processing fee if invoicing through the company; DMCC flexi-desk start-up package AED 43,780 (11 September 2026) — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/2026/09/11/from-salary-to-self-employed-counting-the-cost-of-freelancing-in-the-uae/)
- Entry cost for a legal set-up is "a few thousand dirhams" for someone who already holds a residence visa (11 September 2026) — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/2026/09/11/from-salary-to-self-employed-counting-the-cost-of-freelancing-in-the-uae/)
- Typical documents: passport, photo, CV or portfolio, NOC where applicable (residents on dependent visas or freelancing alongside a job), application forms; licence renews annually, visa/Emirates ID valid two years (29 July 2026) — [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-freelance-licence-visa-costs-requirements-2026/)
- MoHRE issues freelance/self-employment permits not tied to a free zone and valid in all seven emirates; fee estimates conflict (AED 500 for two years vs AED 600 per year) and neither is an official figure (snippet) — [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-freelance-licence-visa-costs-requirements-2026/); [JobXDubai](https://jobxdubai.com/knowledge-hub/visa-immigration/uae-freelance-visa-permit-guide)
- Green Visa for freelancers: needs a MoHRE freelance/self-employment permit, bachelor's degree or specialised diploma, and AED 360,000 annual self-employment income over the previous two years (or proof of solvency); five-year validity (29 July 2026) — [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-freelance-licence-visa-costs-requirements-2026/)

### Inferences
- For a product seller (goods), the Dubai e-Trader licence is the cheapest route found (about AED 1,370/year all-in), but eligibility for expatriates and for goods (vs services) is the unresolved point; freelance permits generally cover listed professional/service activities, not retail trading.
- Enforcement risk for small home sellers appears to be real but uneven: sources cite fines from AED 5,000 to AED 500,000 under different instruments (emirate licensing law vs the federal digital commerce penalty schedule capped at AED 100,000).

### Gaps
- Official current fee schedules from DET (Invest in Dubai), ADDED/TAMM and MoHRE were not retrieved; all fee figures are secondary.
- Whether non-GCC expatriates can currently get the Dubai e-Trader licence, and for which activities, is contradicted across sources and unverified against the official portal.
- No information found on home-business licences in Sharjah, Ajman, RAK, Fujairah or UAQ, or on Abu Dhabi's "Tajer Abu Dhabi" licence fees.
- The full violation-by-violation table of Cabinet Resolution 200 of 2025 (and whether it targets unlicensed individual sellers or mainly platforms) was not read.

## 3. Aani, IBAN transfers and P2P wallets: can individuals take P2P payments for sales, and what limits apply?

### Takeaway
Aani is open to individuals, merchants and corporates through their bank or licensed PSP, with a network cap of AED 50,000 per transaction and lower bank-specific caps in some cases; merchant collection (QR / merchant tag) is offered through business accounts. No rule was found that expressly permits or prohibits individuals taking sale proceeds over P2P, but banks and advisers treat business use of a personal account as grounds for closure.

### Cited Findings
- Aani is operated by Al Etihad Payments, a subsidiary of the CBUAE; available from October 2023 to customers of licensed financial institutions and PSPs, "including individuals, merchants, and corporates"; domestic AED only; settles in under 10 seconds, 24/7 — [Al Etihad Payments FAQ](https://aep.ae/en/frequently-asked-questions/customers/)
- "Aani will support payments of up to 50,000 AED at the initial stage"; features include transfer by proxy (mobile number or email), Request to Pay and QR code payments; fees are set by each institution; positioned as an alternative to debit cards for merchants and online shopping — [Al Etihad Payments FAQ](https://aep.ae/en/frequently-asked-questions/customers/)
- AED 50,000 is the maximum per transaction (snippet) — [Al Etihad Payments, About Aani](https://aep.ae/en/services/about-aani/)
- Bank-level limits differ: CBI states AED 10,000 per transaction and AED 50,000 per day for outward transfers; Emirates Islamic lists AED 10,000 per transaction/account/day; DIB lists AED 50,000 (snippet) — [CBI Aani FAQs](https://www.cbiuae.com/en/personal/contact-and-support/aani/aani-faqs/); [Emirates Islamic](https://www.emiratesislamic.ae/en/priority-banking/ways-to-bank/aani-instant-payment-service); [DIB](https://www.dib.ae/alt/payments-services/aani)
- Merchant side: Mashreq NeoBiz (business account) says merchants can accept payments via QR code or merchant tag without sharing an IBAN and collect up to AED 50,000 per transaction (snippet) — [Mashreq NeoBiz](https://www.mashreq.com/uae/neobiz/banking-solution/forex-transfers/aani)
- Advisers warn freelancers not to invoice through a standard personal bank account and recommend a digital business bank (29 July 2026) — [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-freelance-licence-visa-costs-requirements-2026/)
- Commercial/advisory sources say banks typically respond to business use of a personal account with account closure and, in serious cases, blacklisting; one describes it as illegal under UAE commercial law, a claim not verified against statute (snippet) — [Takween Advisory](https://takweenadvisory.ae/en/blog/corporate-bank-account-freelancers-uae); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/banking/freelancing-in-the-uae-heres-how-to-open-the-right-bank-account-1.500098023)
- CBUAE consumer rules reportedly require 60 calendar days' written notice and reasons before closing a personal account, except where financial crime is suspected (snippet) — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/legalview/uae-when-can-a-bank-shut-a-customers-account-without-prior-notice)
- A freelance-permit holder can generally open a business current account; typical documents are freelance permit, passport/visa, Emirates ID, proof of address, activity description, CV and 3-6 months of bank statements (snippet) — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/banking/freelancing-in-the-uae-heres-how-to-open-the-right-bank-account-1.500098023)

### Inferences
- Technically nothing stops a buyer sending an Aani or IBAN transfer to a seller's personal account; the constraint is contractual (bank account terms) and AML transaction monitoring, not a network-level block. Regular inbound small payments from many unrelated senders is the pattern most likely to trigger review.
- Aani's merchant features (QR, merchant tag, Request to Pay at scale) appear to be delivered through business accounts, which themselves require a licence or freelance permit, so Aani does not by itself remove the licensing prerequisite.

### Gaps
- No Al Etihad Payments or CBUAE source found that states whether individuals may use personal Aani for commercial receipts, or any separate merchant-vs-individual limits. The AEP FAQ still describes AED 50,000 as the "initial stage" cap; whether it changed by November 2026 is unverified.
- P2P wallet limits (e.g. SVF wallet balance/transfer caps by KYC level) were not found.
- The claim that using a personal account for business is "illegal" (rather than a breach of bank terms) is unverified.

## 4. Tax and compliance overhead (VAT, corporate tax for natural persons, e-invoicing)

### Takeaway
Most micro-sellers fall below every tax trigger: VAT registration is mandatory only from AED 375,000 turnover, and corporate tax reaches natural persons only above AED 1 million annual business turnover. E-invoicing becomes mandatory on 1 January 2027 for businesses with revenue of AED 50 million or more and on 1 July 2027 for those below, which is the first obligation likely to touch small licensed sellers.

### Cited Findings
- VAT: registration is mandatory once annual turnover reaches AED 375,000; voluntary registration from AED 187,500 (29 July 2026) — [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-freelance-licence-visa-costs-requirements-2026/)
- Below AED 375,000 turnover, no VAT registration or quarterly returns are needed, but keeping accounting records and issuing formal invoices is advised (updated 11 August 2026) — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/12/29/do-i-need-to-register-a-business-for-home-based-work)
- Corporate tax: under Cabinet Decision No. 49 of 2023 (effective 1 June 2023), natural persons are subject to corporate tax only where turnover from business or business activities exceeds AED 1,000,000 in a Gregorian calendar year (snippet) — [PwC](https://pwc.com/m1/en/services/tax/me-tax-legal-news/2023/uae-ct-cabinet-decision-no-49-2023.html); [Ministry of Finance](https://mof.gov.ae/en/news/ministry-of-finance-announces-issuance-of-uae-cabinet-decision-on-treatment-of-natural-persons-undertaking-a-business-or-business-activity/)
- Wages, personal investment income and real-estate investment income are excluded from the AED 1 million test; freelance income and influencer/artist fees count toward it (snippet) — [Khaleej Times](https://khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-will-9-corporate-tax-apply-to-personal-income-investments-ministry-clarifies?amp=1); [Afridi & Angell](https://afridi-angell.com/uae-cabinet-decision-no-49-of-2023-dated-8-may-2023-effective-1-june-2023)
- Corporate tax rates: 0% on taxable income up to AED 375,000 and 9% above; registration due by 31 March of the year after the AED 1 million threshold is crossed; late registration penalty AED 10,000; keep records seven years (29 July 2026) — [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-freelance-licence-visa-costs-requirements-2026/)
- Small Business Relief (revenue below AED 3 million in the relevant and prior periods) applies only to tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2026 (snippet) — [Khaleej Times](https://khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-will-9-corporate-tax-apply-to-personal-income-investments-ministry-clarifies?amp=1)
- E-invoicing: Ministerial Decision No. 244 of 2025 set the rollout; pilot from 1 July 2026 with voluntary adoption from that date (snippet) — [Ministry of Finance](https://mof.gov.ae/en/news/ministry-of-finance-announces-the-issuance-of-two-ministerial-decisions-on-the-scope-of-obligations-and-the-timelines-for-implementing-the-electronic-invoicing-system-2/); [EY](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2025-1964-uae-ministry-of-finance-publishes-ministerial-decisions-on-e-invoicing)
- Ministerial Resolution No. 66 of 2026 (document dated 14 May 2026) moved the Accredited Service Provider (ASP) appointment deadline for businesses with revenue of AED 50 million or more from 31 July 2026 to 30 October 2026; go-live remains 1 January 2027 (snippet) — [Ministry of Finance, Resolution 66 of 2026](https://mof.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Ministerial-Resolution-No.-66-of-2026-Amending-Certain-Provisions-of-Ministerial-Resolution-No.-244-of-2025-Regarding-the-Implementation-of-the-Electronic-Invoicing-System-En-20260514.pdf); [KPMG, 22 July 2026](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/tnf-uae-phased-implementation-of-e-invoicing-beginning-january-1-2027.html)

- Press framing of the May 2026 change: the delay "gives businesses more time, not less pressure" (snippet) — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business-technology-review/uaes-e-invoicing-delay-gives-businesses-more-time-not-less-pressure)

### Inferences
- An unlicensed or e-Trader-level seller turning over less than AED 375,000 a year has no VAT or corporate tax filing obligation; the practical overhead is record-keeping and (from 1 July 2027, if in scope) e-invoicing through an ASP.
- Payment-provider payout statements become the de facto turnover record against which the AED 375,000 and AED 1 million thresholds would be tested.

### Gaps
- No FTA primary page was opened; the AED 375,000 / 187,500 VAT thresholds are confirmed only via press and advisory sources.
- Whether e-invoicing applies to unregistered natural persons, B2C sales, or sellers below the VAT threshold was not established; the scope exclusions in Decision 243/244 of 2025 were not read. Do not assume micro-sellers are in scope.
- Whether Small Business Relief was extended beyond 31 December 2026 is unverified.

## 5. Transaction or volume caps imposed by regulators or card schemes on individual / unlicensed sellers

### Takeaway
No regulator-set or card-scheme-set transaction or volume cap specific to individual or unlicensed sellers was found. The only hard figures located are Aani's AED 50,000 per-transaction cap (lower at some banks) and the tax thresholds, which are registration triggers rather than payment limits.

### Cited Findings
- Aani network maximum is AED 50,000 per transaction; some banks apply AED 10,000 per transaction or per day (snippet for bank figures) — [Al Etihad Payments FAQ](https://aep.ae/en/frequently-asked-questions/customers/); [CBI Aani FAQs](https://www.cbiuae.com/en/personal/contact-and-support/aani/aani-faqs/)
- Nomod's individual-account documentation states no transaction or volume limits — [Nomod docs](https://nomod.com/docs/account/set-up-account)
- Stripe's UAE activation page states no volume limits and does not offer an unlicensed tier — [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/uae-account-activation-requirements)
- AED 1 million annual turnover is the corporate tax registration trigger for natural persons, not a payments cap (snippet) — [PwC](https://pwc.com/m1/en/services/tax/me-tax-legal-news/2023/uae-ct-cabinet-decision-no-49-2023.html)
- SVF small-facility exemption (float up to AED 500,000 and up to 100 customers) is a provider-level licensing threshold, not a per-seller cap (snippet) — [CBUAE rulebook, SVF Regulation](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/en/entiresection/1536)

### Inferences
- Any caps on individual sellers are most likely set privately by each provider (risk limits, rolling reserves, payout holds) and by acquirer/scheme programme rules, and would need to be collected provider by provider.
- The natural soft ceilings for an individual-tier account are AED 375,000 (VAT) and AED 1 million (corporate tax): above these a provider would reasonably expect tax registration and probably a licence.

### Gaps
- No Visa or Mastercard UAE-specific rule on individual / sole-proprietor / micro-merchant volume thresholds (e.g. payment facilitator sub-merchant limits) was searched or found; this remains open.
- CBUAE rule texts could not be opened, so any limits in the RPSCS or SVF regulations or the payments-risk Guidance are unverified.
- Provider-specific limits for individual accounts (Nomod, Ziina, Mamo, etc.) were not found in public documentation reviewed here.
