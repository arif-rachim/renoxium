# Three UAE fintechs waive the trade licence

Only three UAE payment providers publish an onboarding route for a resident with no trade licence and no freelance permit: **Ziina** (Personal and Professional accounts), **Nomod** (Individual account) and, on weaker evidence, **Mamo** (freelancer route). The best-suited five are completed by **Stripe UAE** and **PayTabs**, which accept a freelance permit in place of a trade licence but do not serve wholly unlicensed sellers. All five pay out in AED to a UAE bank account, and the three licence-free options cost roughly 3.3% to 4.1% of sales on a basket of AED 100 tickets, VAT included. Every other provider reviewed (Telr, Tap, Botim Money, Magnati, Network International, Zbooni) starts its checklist with a trade licence or business legal documents. The important caveat is legal, not commercial: "no trade licence required" is a provider onboarding policy, and every source consulted says selling in the UAE still requires a licence, with fines cited from about AED 5,000 to AED 500,000. The second caveat is evidential: Mamo's help centre blocked direct access, Ziina's own pages disagree on limits and fees, and no Central Bank rule text could be opened, so several cells in the matrix below are marked as unconfirmed. The largest operating risk across these providers is not integration effort but fund holds of 45 days to 540 days after an account review, reported anecdotally by users.

## The field splits into three documentation tiers

The ranking follows one criterion first: what an individual must upload before receiving card payments and cashing out to a UAE bank account. Tier one needs an Emirates ID only, tier two needs a freelance permit, and tier three needs a trade licence.

**Ziina is the clearest tier-one option.** Its help centre states that on a Professional account "no Trade License is required to get started" and that the account is available immediately, while the verified Business tier requires a trade licence ([Ziina help centre](https://ziina.com/help-center/6847490-what-is-the-difference-between-personal-professional-and-business-accounts)). Ziina is also the only one of the five with press-confirmed Central Bank licensing for this activity: a Stored Value Facility licence reported in June 2024 ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/ziina-becomes-first-venture-backed-start-up-to-secure-uae-central-bank-svf-license-ovmyunso)) and a Retail Payment Services licence covering merchant acquiring reported in October 2026 ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/fintech/news/ziina-secures-cbuae-retail-payment-services-licence)). The exact documents Ziina collects for a Professional account were not found on any page that could be opened.

**Nomod publishes the most explicit document list.** Its Individual account is for "Freelancers with freelancer permits, consultants, or anyone selling without a trade license", and asks for Emirates ID, proof of address and bank account details, with a freelance permit only "if you have one". Review "typically takes around 48 hours", and no volume cap for individuals is stated ([Nomod docs](https://nomod.com/docs/account/set-up-account)). No source on Nomod's regulatory licence was found.

**Mamo probably belongs in tier one, but the evidence conflicts.** Search extracts of its help centre say a trade licence "is not required if you are a freelancer, i.e. you do not yet have a registered business", with Emirates ID and proof of address mandatory ([Mamo help: Business documents](https://help.mamopay.com/en/articles/9932398-business-documents)). Another extract says onboarding collects "your trade licence and MOA" from "businesses and freelancers registered and operating in the UAE" ([Mamo help: Getting started](https://help.mamopay.com/en/articles/7200023-getting-started-with-mamo-business)). Both pages returned HTTP 403 when opened, so neither statement was read in context. Mamo's only confirmed registration is with the Dubai Financial Services Authority ([DFSA register](https://dfsa.ae/public-register/firms/mamo-limited)).

**Stripe and PayTabs sit in tier two.** Stripe requires a "Trade license or freelancer permit issued within the UAE" for every business type ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/uae-account-activation-requirements)) and states that it does not support individuals without one ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/what-types-of-businesses-can-open-a-uae-account)). PayTabs runs a freelancer route that needs a freelancer certificate, passport, Emirates ID and residence permit, plus a bank statement ([PayTabs](https://ai.paytabs.com/en/get-onboarded-with-your-freelancer-certificate/)). These two matter because a freelance permit is far cheaper than a commercial licence: an Abu Dhabi freelancer licence is reported at AED 790 to AED 1,200 a year ([Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-freelance-licence-visa-costs-requirements-2026/)).

The providers left out fail the documentation test. Botim Money's simplified tier still lists a trade licence and caps acceptance at AED 6,000 a day ([Botim Money pricing](https://www.botim.money/pricing)). Telr requires a trade licence even for sole proprietors ([Telr checklist](https://telr.com/documents-checklist)), and Tap requires a business licence or commercial registration plus a live website ([Tap support](https://support.tap.company/en/support/solutions/articles/153000242639)). PayPal and Payoneer were excluded because no official source on their UAE onboarding documents for individuals was found.

## Nomod's rates undercut the field on small tickets

Fixed fees decide the ranking for micro-merchants. At an AED 50 ticket, a AED 1 fixed fee adds two percentage points, which lifts Ziina to about 4.6% and Stripe or Mamo Growth to about 4.9%, while Nomod's links land near 4.0% to 4.3% on the same arithmetic. In-person sellers do better still: **Nomod's Tap to Pay carries no fixed fee and starts at 1.67% for standard cards**, less than half the cost of a payment link at that ticket size ([Nomod standard plans](https://nomod.com/docs/standard-plans)).

Speed has an explicit price. Mamo charges +0.75% per transaction for same-day settlement on its free plan, which makes its fast rate about 3.65% + AED 1 ([Mamo pricing](https://www.mamopay.com/pricing)). Nomod's next-day plan costs 0.3 points more than its five-day plan, and Stripe's instant payouts cost 1.5% ([Stripe UAE pricing](https://stripe.com/ae/pricing)). Ziina's standard cashout arrives in one to two business days ([Ziina blog](https://ziina.com/blog/how-can-i-collect-payments-without-extensive-paperwork)).

Ziina's published numbers are the least settled of the five, and three conflicts stand open. First, the business rate is 2.6% + AED 1 on a page updated June 2026, while an older help article still shows 2.9% + AED 1 ([Ziina older fee article](https://ziina.com/help-center/6847430-what-are-the-fees-to-use-ziina-business)). Second, on Personal accounts the payer, not the seller, bears 2.9% + AED 1 ([Ziina fees](https://ziina.com/fees)), and no page states which model applies to Professional accounts. Third, one help article says cashouts are free ([Ziina cash-out article](https://ziina.com/help-center/6847423-do-i-pay-a-fee-to-cash-out)) while another extract mentions AED 1 per cashout ([Ziina help centre](https://ziina.com/help-center/12781259-what-are-the-fees-to-use-ziina-business)).

Ziina's limits conflict too. The account-comparison page gives Personal and Professional accounts AED 50,000 per card transaction and AED 50,000 received per calendar month, fixed ([Ziina help centre](https://ziina.com/help-center/6847490-what-is-the-difference-between-personal-professional-and-business-accounts)). A separate Ziina article, seen only as an extract, caps card receipts on Personal accounts at AED 1,500 per rolling seven days ([Ziina card limits article](https://ziina.com/help-center/15136431-whats-the-difference-between-the-1-500-aed-and-20-000-aed-limits)). If the lower figure applies, a Personal account is unusable for regular selling and Professional is the real entry point.

Disputes are the hidden cost for small sellers. Only Stripe (AED 60) and Nomod (AED 277) publish a chargeback fee, and either figure exceeds a typical micro-merchant sale.

## Links take minutes while platforms hit a licence wall

For a solo seller, integration is close to zero work on all five. Ziina, Nomod, Mamo, Stripe and PayTabs each offer payment links that need no code. Ziina adds QR codes, tap to pay and an official WooCommerce plugin, and its API tests with a simple `test: true` flag, though it exposes only one webhook event ([Ziina docs](https://docs.ziina.com/api-reference/webhook)). Mamo gives each merchant a full sandbox but only after account verification ([Mamo help: API integrations](https://help.mamopay.com/en/articles/7234144-api-integrations)). PayTabs is the only one whose documentation maps its hosted page to the lightest card-security self-assessment ([PayTabs docs](https://docs.paytabs.com/PT2-API-Endpoints/Integration-Types-Manuals/Hosted-Payment-Page/HPP-Step-1-Setup-and-Activate-Integration-Method/HPP-Step-1-Landing)). Nomod's developer API was not researched.

The picture changes for a platform that wants to onboard many unlicensed sellers or gig workers under its own account. **No provider was found that officially permits this.** Stripe Connect requires "a valid trade license issued within the UAE" for every connected account ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/connect-availability-in-the-uae)), and Telr's split-payment feature asks each sub-merchant for a trade licence ([Telr docs](https://docs.telr.com/reference/onboarding-submerchant)). The workable pattern is for a licensed platform to collect funds itself and pay individuals by bank transfer. **Mamo is the only provider with a published price for that: AED 5 per domestic payout** ([Mamo payouts](https://www.mamopay.com/business/payouts)), which is 5% of a AED 100 payout and 0.5% of a AED 1,000 one, so batching earnings matters. What identity checks the platform must run on each recipient was not documented in any source found.

Aani, the Central Bank's instant-payment rail, is the zero-integration fallback. Any bank customer can request money through a banking app, funds settle in under 10 seconds, and the cap is AED 50,000 per payment ([Al Etihad Payments FAQ](https://aep.ae/en/frequently-asked-questions/small-merchants-and-sole-proprietors/)). It takes no cards, and merchant QR features are delivered through business accounts that themselves need a licence or permit.

## Lenient onboarding does not remove the licence duty

The provider's leniency and the law are separate matters. A trade licence is required to run any kind of business in the UAE, with no exception for size or turnover, and fines start around AED 5,000 ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/12/29/do-i-need-to-register-a-business-for-home-based-work)). An older legal commentary cites up to AED 500,000 for unlicensed social-media selling ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/up-to-dh500000-fine-for-selling-without-licence-on-social-media-in-uae)). Ziina says so itself: customers must "ensure they hold any trade license, permit, tax registration, or other approval" their activity requires ([Ziina blog](https://ziina.com/blog/how-can-i-collect-payments-without-extensive-paperwork)). No Central Bank rule defining an "individual seller" tier was found, and the rule texts could not be opened, so the tier appears to be a provider risk decision. That reading is an inference.

Three recurring overheads follow from that position.

- **Bank-side risk:** advisers warn that banks respond to business use of a personal account with closure ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/banking/freelancing-in-the-uae-heres-how-to-open-the-right-bank-account-1.500098023)). Payouts from all five providers must go to an account in the holder's own name where the rule is stated.
- **Fund holds:** an independent review citing Ziina's FAQ says Ziina may hold funds 45 to 120 days on termination ([paymentreview.com](https://paymentreview.com/reviews/ziina/)). Trustpilot reviewers report holds extended to 540 days at Mamo ([Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/mamopay.com)). These reports are self-selected and unverified, but the pattern repeats across providers.
- **Renewals:** Stripe pauses charges and payouts if an expired permit or Emirates ID is not renewed by its deadline ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/continuous-verification-in-the-uae)).

Tax overhead is light at this scale. VAT registration becomes mandatory at AED 375,000 of annual turnover ([Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-freelance-licence-visa-costs-requirements-2026/)), and corporate tax reaches individuals only above AED 1 million ([PwC](https://pwc.com/m1/en/services/tax/me-tax-legal-news/2023/uae-ct-cabinet-decision-no-49-2023.html)). Whether the July 2027 e-invoicing deadline applies to sellers below the VAT threshold was not established.

## Conclusion

The choice reduces to how much legal exposure the seller accepts. Ziina and Nomod let an unlicensed resident take card payments this week, with Nomod cheaper on published rates and Ziina better licensed and faster to start. Neither makes the selling lawful. The more durable route is a low-cost licence: Dubai's e-Trader licence is reported at AED 1,070 plus a AED 300 chamber fee a year ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/12/29/do-i-need-to-register-a-business-for-home-based-work)), though sources conflict on whether expatriates and goods sellers qualify. At AED 10,000 of monthly sales that licence costs about a third of one year's processing fees, and it unlocks Stripe, PayTabs and business bank accounts while removing the reason most holds begin.

Before committing, three points need checking directly with the providers because the public record is contradictory or blocked: Ziina's Professional-account fee model and card-receipt limit, Mamo's acceptance of an applicant with only an Emirates ID, and Nomod's regulatory status and any unpublished cap on Individual accounts. The legal position should be confirmed with the relevant emirate's economic department; nothing here is legal advice.
